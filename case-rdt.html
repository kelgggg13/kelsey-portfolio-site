Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) Reader App

Team
PM, front-end eng, full-stack eng, ML eng
Timeframe
June – Dec 2020 (6 months)
My role
UX Research & Design Lead (sole designer)
Skills
Research, personas, affinity mapping, wireframing

I was UX Research and Design Lead, the sole designer on a graduate capstone sponsored by Microsoft AI for Good and PATH, owning the process end to end from discovery through high-fidelity design and handoff.

Problem

Rapid diagnostic tests are widely used across low- and middle-income countries, but result interpretation is inconsistent. Operators read weak test lines differently, and that variability feeds into the national and international health data used for policy decisions.

How might we improve RDT interpretation consistency and efficiency for lab technicians, and eventually community health workers in LMICs, using AI and ubiquitous devices?

Approach

None of us had prior healthcare experience, so I started with secondary research to build a shared baseline: a review of 70 publications and sources, plus a competitive analysis of 6 existing products. From there I ran 9 expert interviews across lab technicians, community health workers, and RDT researchers, and led the team through affinity mapping to synthesize what we heard.

Image: secondary research insights + affinity map

That research pointed to two distinct user groups, lab technicians and community health workers, who had different training and different working environments but shared a need to support both single and batch testing. I turned those findings into personas and a journey map, led the team through a prioritized PRD, and then designed the information architecture myself.

Image: personas + journey map

Design started on paper. Wireframing by hand let me iterate quickly with engineering and product in real time, without getting attached too early. From there I went through three rounds of mid-to-high-fidelity design, each one tested against pre-alpha and alpha feedback. The current version is built around a Templates to Current Tests to History flow meant to improve data-collection efficiency for lab technicians, and all of it has been fully implemented in Android and Flutter.

Image: paper wireframes → high-fidelity screens

Research validation

I led 11 in-person usability studies (6 RDT experts, 2 non-experts) at PATH's Seattle office, run under realistic conditions, participants wore gloves throughout to mimic actual field use. We mapped and prioritized the findings to guide the next design round ahead of a planned evaluation in December.

Image: usability testing sessions + findings map

Takeaways

This was the first time I owned an end-to-end UX process on my own, from research design through implementation handoff, and it taught me to design inside real technical and business constraints instead of defaulting to open-ended exploration. Leading research in a space none of us knew well also shaped how I approach ambiguous problems now: build the evidence first, and let it drive every decision that follows, from personas to IA to the interaction design itself.
